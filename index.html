Our purpose

A world-class middle school education, close to home.

Meridian Honors Academy was designed in response to families seeking stronger academic preparation, deeper learning, and clearer pathways to demanding high school programs—without leaving their community.

Mission

Meridian Honors Academy prepares students for advanced high school pathways and empowered civic participation through rigorous instruction, structured student supports, culturally responsive curriculum, and research connected to community and global inquiry.

Vision

A rigorous and humane public middle school where every student is seen, known, and challenged—and graduates academically accelerated, culturally grounded, and prepared to thrive.
01

Academic excellence and equity reinforce each other.

All students deserve access to challenging work and the support needed to achieve at high levels.

02

Community is a source of knowledge.

Crown Heights becomes a living laboratory for history, science, culture, public policy, and civic inquiry.

03

Students should produce, not only consume, knowledge.

Research, exhibitions, publications, and public presentations make learning meaningful and accountable.

Signature programs

Three connected experiences. One coherent model.

Research generates questions. Residencies provide authentic contexts. Civic leadership turns evidence into action.

01

Meridian Research Institute

Students learn to formulate questions, evaluate sources, collect and analyze evidence, write for authentic audiences, and communicate findings with confidence.

  • Community inquiry in Grade 6
  • Applied research and publication in Grade 7
  • Independent thesis and oral defense in Grade 8
02

Residency Program

Learning extends beyond the classroom through structured partnerships with libraries, cultural institutions, colleges, community organizations, research centers, and professional partners.

  • Neighborhood fieldwork and oral history
  • Institutional research and cultural investigation
  • Career and civic immersion
03

Civic Leadership Program

Students examine community, national, and global issues through multiple perspectives, then use research to inform public scholarship, advocacy, and action.

  • Community issues forums
  • Youth action projects
  • Public defense and action campaigns

Rigor with access

High expectations never mean going it alone.

Meridian’s support systems are designed to help students remain fully included in rigorous core instruction while receiving timely, targeted help.

A

Strong universal instruction

Inquiry-based, culturally responsive classrooms use discussion, writing, research, formative feedback, and Universal Design for Learning.

B

Targeted intervention

Small-group literacy, mathematics, academic language, executive functioning, attendance, and study-skills supports supplement core learning.

C

Individualized pathways

Students with significant needs receive intensive, documented plans coordinated with IEP, ENL, counseling, and family supports where appropriate.

A school where students are known

Advisory, mentorship, and regular data reviews help adults notice barriers early and respond proactively.

Crown Heights as classroom

Study the community. Learn from its people. Contribute to its future.

Students investigate local history, culture, demographics, public policy, economic development, public health, housing, and community assets through recurring research and residency experiences.

Families, libraries, colleges, cultural institutions, community organizations, and civic leaders become partners in the educational process—not occasional visitors.

Admissions overview

A new public middle school option for New York City families.

Meridian Honors Academy is proposed to serve students in Grades 6–8 at a Crown Heights campus. The current design anticipates applications from students across New York City Community School Districts, with focused outreach in nearby Brooklyn communities.
